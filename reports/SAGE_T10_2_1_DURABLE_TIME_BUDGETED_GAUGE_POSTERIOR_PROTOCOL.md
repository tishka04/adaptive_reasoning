## Durable collection transaction

Before every environment step, the collector atomically writes and flushes a
canonical `ActionIntent` containing its manifest checksum, lane, reset, step,
structural action schema, opaque grounding hash, and intent checksum.  Raw
action arguments are never persisted.  After the step it atomically writes an
`ActionReceipt` linked to that intent and, when available, the sealed physical
event checksum.  Lane state and the resumable collection checkpoint use the
same write-temporary, flush, `fsync`, replace discipline.

For every reset, lane, and the global journal, reconstruction must establish:

`issued_intents = sealed_events + explicitly_unresolved_intents`

Every unresolved intent is terminal for its reset, carries a registered
timeout or worker-loss reason, counts against the legal-action budget, and is
excluded from likelihood and posterior updates.  Every intent identifier and
event identifier is unique.  A resumed worker may continue only from the last
signed checkpoint.  It closes an interrupted reset conservatively and starts
the next registered reset; it never physically replays a partial reset,
repeats a completed step, or discards an issued intent.

The compact root `collection_report.json` contains a `durability` summary and
content descriptors for the local journal, lane reports, and checkpoint.  The
underlying journal and checkpoint remain local omitted artifacts.  They are
never treated as transferable model inputs.

Unknown or unclassified action accounting, a missing intent, an orphan
receipt, a checksum mismatch, or a noncanonical durability record yields
`DATA_OR_PROVENANCE_INVALID`.  A fully attested but incomplete reset, lane, or
collection caused by a registered timeout or resource stop yields
`SOURCE_ACQUISITION_OR_RESOURCE_MISS`.  Neither outcome permits fit or a retry
under the same manifest.

## Nested time budgets

All authoritative durations use externally sampled monotonic clocks bound to
the manifest.  Wall-clock timestamps are descriptive only.

| Scope | Cooperative stop | Hard stop | Required behavior |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| each reset | 55 s | 60 s | stop issuing intents, settle, flush, then refuse |
| each four-reset lane | — | 250 s | persist a signed lane result before returning |
| complete source collection | 5,100 s | 5,400 s | close all ledgers and reports |

The eighteen 250-second lane ceilings total 4,500 s.  The cooperative global
limit therefore reserves 600 s for environment initialization, atomic
persistence, cross-fit bookkeeping, and compaction.  The interval from 5,100
to 5,400 s is a closure reserve, not additional collection time.  At 5,100 s
no new lane or action intent may begin.

A reset timeout seals that reset and the collector continues with the next
registered reset when its durable boundary is safe.  A lane timeout seals that
lane and collection continues with the next registered lane.  These failures
remain visible in the compact reports and prevent a source-gate pass; they do
not silently discard the remaining lanes.  Only a global deadline, a resource
gate, or a provenance error stops the complete collection immediately.

An outer process watchdog may enforce 5,400 s, but it is not accepted as a
substitute for the signed monotonic timing proof.  Reaching the outer watchdog
without a reconstructible report is a provenance failure.

## Kaggle deployment is not a scientific gate

The current deployment target is a nine-hour, 110-private-game Kaggle run.
Nine hours is 32,400 s, or 294.5 s per game before startup, finalization, and
load imbalance.  This fact motivates durable per-reset and per-lane deadlines,
but it does not alter the six-game scientific split, paired validation, source
thresholds, or verdict.

T10.2.1 scientific support does not establish 110-game throughput.  Conversely,
finishing a Kaggle notebook within nine hours does not support the causal
hypothesis.  A future frozen deployment audit must report a separate
`kaggle_deployment_status`; hidden-game outcomes may not be used to retune
T10.2.1.
